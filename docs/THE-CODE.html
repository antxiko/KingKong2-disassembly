The code

Everything runs in the interrupt

The cartridge hooks H.TIMI to p00:4229, which runs once per frame: first the sound, with banks 4 and 5 mapped (p04:6228), and then the game (p01:65C2): it bumps the frame counter (0xC103) and branches on the state.

The dispatcher

p00:425F is Konami's dispatcher: the table of destinations is glued right after the call. It pops the return address, which is the base of the table, and jumps to destination number A. p00:4260 does the same with the table in HL. The listing marks each of those tables as data and names their destinations.

The game states

0xC100 is the state and 0xC101 its step (table at p01:65D6): the Konami logo being uncovered, the demo, the title, the game, GAME OVER with its CONTINUE (F5), the pause and the ending (p06:A6CF). The game itself (p00:5C05) branches again on 0xC201: playing, changing screen, in a conversation, the durian&hellip;

A screen

On entering a screen (0xC125), p00:4A7B chooses its tile set (a nibble per screen at p07:6009, or per plane at p07:6000) and uploads to the page 1 sheet what its list says. The map is 5 × 5 blocks of 4 × 4 tiles (p10:6000, or p04:7E76 on the alternate planes); p00:4EE7 copies the 20 × 20 tiles to page 0 with HMMM from (8, 8). Then the palette, the screen's sprites (p00:4CB3), the enemies (the list at p13:6000) and what is painted on top: the statue, the bosses, the scenery that can be removed.

Twelve screens are split: p06:BEC5 shows one plane or the other depending on which side Mitchel is on (0xC131).

Mitchel

His state is 0xC300 (table at p01:6D3C): 0 still, 1-4 walking up, down, left and right, 5 the hit, 6 spinning, 7 throwing. He walks from one 8-dot tile to the next (p01:6F17); in the water of tile set 4, without the clogs, at half step (p01:6F00). He is two overlaid 16 × 16 sprites; his pose comes every frame from an RLE at p13:7D9B.

The enemies

Thirteen records of 0x80 bytes at 0xD000. The type goes in ix+0: p01:6885 says which routine runs every frame and p00:5B1E what it does when created. Nearly all of them go by steps (ix+1) through the dispatcher. From ix+0x12 on, each type uses the record its own way; tools/anota.py knows what each field is in each enemy's code and comments it.

What gets thrown (stones, balls, the boomerang) are other records, the figures 0x81-0x91 (p01:7077). On dying, p03:B9E6 decides what each type drops, with its own count at 0xDFC0-0xDFDB.

The sound

p04:6032 receives the sound: below 0x80, an effect, which uses the PSG's channel C and takes it from the music while it plays; from 0x80 to 0x8D, one of the 14 pieces, with one track per channel (p04:66D8). 0xFD pauses (and plays a little tune), 0xFE brings the music back and 0xFF fades it out. The tracks are bytes of note, rest, tempo, octave, volume, envelope, jumps, calls and repeats (p04:638A).

How the listing is made

tools/bancos.py traces the whole cartridge following the bank switches, the dispatcher and the bridges. tools/mkasm.py writes each bank with its org, its labels and its comments, taken from src/pNN.notes. 100% of the 131,072 bytes is assigned to code (37,983) or data (93,089), and 40.1% of the 18,093 instructions carry a comment, with no routine below 10%.
